The Tortoise and the Hare

Long ago, in a green valley, there lived a hare who loved to brag about his speed. Every morning he stood on the hill and shouted to the animals below.

“I am the fastest creature alive! Nobody can catch me!”

One day, a slow old tortoise looked up from the grass and smiled.

“I will race you,” said the tortoise.

The hare laughed so hard he fell over. “You? Race me? This will be the easiest win of my life!”

A wise fox agreed to judge the race. He drew a line in the dirt and raised his paw. “Ready, set, go!”

The hare shot off like an arrow and was soon far out of sight. The tortoise kept walking, one careful step after another.

Halfway along the road, the hare stopped and looked back. There was no tortoise anywhere.

“He is still miles behind me,” the hare said with a yawn. “I have plenty of time for a nap.”

He lay down under a shady tree and closed his eyes.

Hours passed. The tortoise never stopped. Step by step, he crept past the sleeping hare, and he kept going until the finish line was close.

At last the hare woke up with a start. He saw the tortoise just a few steps from the end.

“Wait!” cried the hare. “No!”

He ran as fast as he could, but it was too late. The tortoise crossed the line, and the animals cheered.

“Slow and steady wins the race,” said the tortoise gently.

The hare hung his head. “I should have listened to my own legs, not my own pride,” he said quietly.
